# Magnus Workspace Platform — pilot ground rules

**For the pilot group. 4 September 2026.**

The platform is open for you to use. It is not finished. This page says exactly
what is safe to rely on, what is not, and what we need back from you.

Read the three rules first. They are short and they matter.

---

## The three rules

### 1. The platform is a rehearsal. Today's real process still runs.

For the pilot period, everything you do in the platform is **also** done the way
you do it now — the same Messenger message, the same signed form, the same
spreadsheet. The platform is a second copy running alongside, not a replacement.

If the platform and the current process disagree, **the current process is
correct.**

This is not caution for its own sake. It is how you find out whether the platform
actually fits the work, without the company depending on the answer.

### 2. An approval inside the platform is not an approval.

The approval machinery is built but not yet connected. Right now:

- The platform does not check who is allowed to approve what.
- Approval requests are not routed to anyone. Nothing lands in anyone's queue.
- A record can show "approved" without any person having approved it.

So: **nothing you approve in the platform is authorised.** A write-off, a
purchase order, a fund request, a progress claim — if it needs approval today, it
still needs that approval today, through the channel you use now. Getting it
"approved" in the platform means nothing has happened.

Do not point at a platform record as evidence that something was authorised.

### 3. Safety records in the platform authorise nothing. No exceptions.

The platform will currently let anyone issue a permit to work, lift a safety
stop, or close an incident investigation. It does not check that the person is
the Safety Officer. That check is specified and is not yet built.

**A permit to work that exists only in the platform is not a permit to work.**
Nobody does hot work, works at height, or works on live electrical on the
strength of a platform record. The paper permit and the Safety Officer's decision
are the only things that authorise site work, exactly as today.

This rule has no exceptions and does not depend on how convincing the screen
looks. If you are unsure, the answer is the paper permit.

## What is worth testing hard

These parts are built and behave. Push them.

**Messages and chat.** This is the reason the platform exists — it replaces the
Messenger groups. Use it for real conversation. Start groups, invite people,
comment on records, mention colleagues. Tell us where it is slower or more
awkward than Messenger, because that is the thing that decides whether people
move.

**Finding things.** Customers, sites, people, projects, items, locations. Search
for someone by nickname. Search for a site by the town rather than its formal
name. Tell us every time you could not find something you knew was there.

**Creating the records you create anyway.** Customers and contacts, sites,
opportunities, projects, tasks, site reports, stock and item records. The object
layer works. This is where we find out whether the fields match the job.

**Files and Google Drive.** Upload photographs and documents the way you would.
Check they arrive and that you can get them back.

**On your phone, on site, on bad signal.** Especially in Sorsogon and Dumaguete.
If it does not work on a phone at a site, it does not work.

---

## What to leave alone for now

Not because it is dangerous to look at, but because anything you enter will
probably have to be re-entered later.

- Payroll
- Anything under approvals and gates
- Permits to work, safety stops, incident closure — see rule 3

---

## Your data may not survive

Several corrections are coming that change how records are stored: approval gate
numbers are being reseeded, some status values are being renamed, and customer
accounts are being separated from contracting parties.

**Treat everything you enter during the pilot as disposable.** Enter it properly
— sloppy test data teaches us nothing — but do not spend a day carefully
migrating a year of history into it yet. That comes after the corrections, and it
will be done by import, not by typing.

If you build something you would hate to lose, tell us and we will make sure it
survives.

---

## What we need back from you

More useful than "it's good" or "it's slow". For anything that goes wrong or
feels wrong, five lines:

```
WHAT I WAS DOING:
WHAT I EXPECTED:
WHAT HAPPENED:
WHERE:            (screen name, and the record if there is one)
DEVICE / SIGNAL:  (laptop, phone; office, site, weak signal)
```

Four things are worth reporting even when nothing is broken:

1. **Somewhere the platform made you do more work than the old way.** Not "it is
   different" — genuinely more steps, more typing, more waiting. Say which.
2. **A field you had to leave blank, or fill with something untrue** because the
   real answer did not fit. That is a design error and we want it.
3. **Something you needed and could not find at all.**
4. **A word that is wrong.** A label that is not what the job is called at
   Magnus, or an abbreviation where the full term belongs.

Send it however is fastest — but if the platform's own messaging is working for
you, send it there. That is itself a test.

---

## What happens next

The pilot runs, we collect what you find, and it is combined with the technical
conformance list already open with the builder. Together those become the
milestones and deliverables for the next round — which means what you report in
the next week genuinely shapes what gets built.

Pilot group, please ask if any of this is unclear. Especially rule 3.
