# Part 1 of 10 — The Company on One Page

**Level-0 cross-functional process landscape · as specified · 8 September 2026**
**Companion file:** `Part 1 — The Company on One Page (A3 landscape).pdf` in the same folder

---

## 1. What this page is for

One sheet that shows the whole company as the platform models it: **who acts, in what order, and
where the company has decided a second person must say yes or the platform must say no.** Every
gate and every hard block is placed on the step it governs. Nothing is summarised away — if a
control exists in the specification, it is on this page.

It is a landscape, not a procedure. Parts 2 to 9 open each column into its steps, records, states
and hand-offs. This page is the index to them and the thing to pin on a wall.

**Read it two ways.** Across a lane: everything one department does, in the order value moves.
Down a column: everyone who touches one phase, and which lanes are silent in it — the empty cells
are as informative as the full ones.

## 4. The thirty gates, placed

A gate is a decision a named role must make. On the page each is a green badge showing the gate
number, the primary approver (**Accountable**), the alternate (**Consulted**), and the working-day
window where the specification sets one.

| Column | Gates on the page | Who holds them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Win the Work | **G6** proposal release · **G7** below-policy markup · **G9** signed agreement · **G10** contract risk review | Director ×3 · Chief Executive Officer ×1 |
| Design and Permit | **G18** design release for permitting · **G20** permit consultant | Design Engineer · Chief Operating Officer |
| Buy and Store | **G4** order ≤ ₱100,000 · **G5** order > ₱100,000 · **G23** fund release · **G25a** inter-island transfer · **G25b** within-island > ₱100,000 · **G26** variance write-off · **G27** opening stock lock | Procurement Head · Director ×2 · Head of Finance · Project Manager · Warehouse Custodian ×2 |
| Build Safely | **G1 / G2 / G3** write-off by band · **G8** variation order · **G11** progress claim · **G19** non-conformance closure · **G28** permit to work · **G29** safety stop lift · **G30** incident closure | Person In Charge · Project Manager ×2 · Chief Operating Officer ×2 · Director · Head of Finance · Safety Officer ×2 |
| Commission and Hand Over | **G12** retention invoice · **G21** turnover date | Head of Finance · Director |
| Operate and Maintain | **G34** warranty claim | Head of Finance |
| Govern | **G22** system constant · **G24** role assignment · **G31** thresholds · **G32** role permissions · **G33** document classification | Chief Operating Officer ×2 · Director · Chief Executive Officer · Document Controller |

**Two things the placement makes visible that a list does not.**

*Build Safely carries nine gates.* It is the densest column on the page because it is where money,
safety and quality all move at once — and it is where sixty-three people will spend most of their
time. Part 5 is the longest part for that reason.

*Safety Officer holds two gates with no alternate.* Permits to work and safety stop lifts cannot be
approved by anyone else. That is correct — safety approvals are never delegated — and it means a
site with its Safety Officer on leave cannot issue a permit to work that day. The specification
accepts that consequence deliberately.

## 5. The six hard blocks, placed

A hard block is not a decision. Nobody can waive it. The step cannot happen until the precondition
exists. On the page each is a red box with a stop sign, placed in the lane and column where it bites.

| Block | Where on the page | What it stops |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **HB1** insurance certificate | Projects and Site · Build | Mobilisation |
| **HB2** safety and health program | Safety · Build | The first construction activity |
| **HB3** block B0 signed off | Projects and Site · Build | The first electrical block |
| **HB4** prerequisite permit | Projects and Site · Build | Mobilisation |
| **HB5** quarantined material | Procurement and Inventory · Build | Issue to site |
| **HB6** signed contract | Projects and Site · Win — and Finance and Payroll · Buy | Leaving setup · any purchase order · any fund request |

HB6 appears twice because it guards two different lanes: the project cannot leave setup, and the
money cannot move. Both are the same rule.

Four of the six sit at the start of Build. **The specification treats mobilisation as the point of
no return** — insurance, permit, safety program and contract all have to exist before the first
person is deployed. That is the single most important thing this page says about how the company
is meant to run.

## 6. Where the platform acts on its own

The bottom lane is the one a paper process map never has. Reading across it:

- **Every write is appended to a hash-chained audit trail and numbered automatically.** Nothing can
  be edited out of the history — a fact worth knowing before the first test record is created.
- **Orders and fund requests are refused without a signed contract** — HB6 enforced by the system,
  not by a person remembering.
- **HB1 to HB5 fire at the step they guard**, and a site report cannot be submitted without that
  day's toolbox meeting — the rule that makes the safety record a precondition of the production
  record.
- **The protocol reads, writes and decides under scoped tokens**, and Administration is reachable
  only from a browser.

And one amber note: **nothing notifies anyone.** Every "Informed" entry in the RACI tables of Parts
2–9 is what the specification intends, not what happens today.

## 7. What is deliberately not on this page

- **Steps, records and states.** Each box on the page is a chain that Parts 2–9 open into its
  individual steps with the record and state each one moves.
- **The RACI rows.** The page shows who is Accountable (the gate badge). Responsible, Consulted and
  Informed for every step are in the part that owns it.
- **Hand-offs.** Arrows are implied left to right. Every named hand-off — the places a process
  actually stalls — is drawn in the detail parts.
- **Messages.** It is a channel, not a process, and it appears wherever a step's record has a
  thread. It has no lane of its own.

## 8. Where the live build differs from this page today

The amber banner at the foot of the page lists five. Each is marked at its exact step in Parts 2–9
and each traces to a finding in the sweep reports of 5–6 September.

| | Deviation | Effect on this page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **RC-A** | One same-person check in sixteen approval controls | Every green badge on the page can today be satisfied by the person who raised the record |
| **RC-B** | Write-off gate chosen from a dropdown | G1, G2 and G3 are selected, not derived from the amount |
| **Register** | 25 of 30 gate labels and all 6 hard blocks on the live rows predate this specification | No approver is named on screen for any badge; the six red boxes enforce a different six rules than the ones drawn |
| **RC-H** | Nothing notifies anyone | The Informed column across the series is design intent |
| **RC-N** | Live "Hard Block 6" blocks fund requests while an advance is unliquidated | The HB6 drawn on this page — no contract, no orders, no funds — is not the rule the platform runs |

**None of these change the drawing.** They are marks. When the Monday fixes land and the register
is reconciled, the marks come off and the page stands unchanged — which is why it is drawn as
specified.
