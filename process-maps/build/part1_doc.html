1. What this page is for

One sheet that shows the whole company as the platform models it: who acts, in what order, and where the company has decided a second person must say yes or the platform must say no. Every gate and every hard block is placed on the step it governs. Nothing is summarised away — if a control exists in the specification, it is on this page.

It is a landscape, not a procedure. Parts 2 to 9 open each column into its steps, records, states and hand-offs. This page is the index to them and the thing to pin on a wall.

Read it two ways. Across a lane: everything one department does, in the order value moves. Down a column: everyone who touches one phase, and which lanes are silent in it — the empty cells are as informative as the full ones.

Two things the placement makes visible that a list does not.

Build Safely carries nine gates. It is the densest column on the page because it is where money, safety and quality all move at once — and it is where sixty-three people will spend most of their time. Part 5 is the longest part for that reason.

Safety Officer holds two gates with no alternate. Permits to work and safety stop lifts cannot be approved by anyone else. That is correct — safety approvals are never delegated — and it means a site with its Safety Officer on leave cannot issue a permit to work that day. The specification accepts that consequence deliberately.

5. The six hard blocks, placed

A hard block is not a decision. Nobody can waive it. The step cannot happen until the precondition exists. On the page each is a red box with a stop sign, placed in the lane and column where it bites.

BlockWhere on the pageWhat it stops
HB1 insurance certificateProjects and Site · BuildMobilisation
HB2 safety and health programSafety · BuildThe first construction activity
HB3 block B0 signed offProjects and Site · BuildThe first electrical block
HB4 prerequisite permitProjects and Site · BuildMobilisation
HB5 quarantined materialProcurement and Inventory · BuildIssue to site
HB6 signed contractProjects and Site · Win — and Finance and Payroll · BuyLeaving setup · any purchase order · any fund request

HB6 appears twice because it guards two different lanes: the project cannot leave setup, and the money cannot move. Both are the same rule.

Four of the six sit at the start of Build. The specification treats mobilisation as the point of no return — insurance, permit, safety program and contract all have to exist before the first person is deployed. That is the single most important thing this page says about how the company is meant to run.

6. Where the platform acts on its own

The bottom lane is the one a paper process map never has. Reading across it:

  • Every write is appended to a hash-chained audit trail and numbered automatically. Nothing can be edited out of the history — a fact worth knowing before the first test record is created.
  • Orders and fund requests are refused without a signed contract — HB6 enforced by the system, not by a person remembering.
  • HB1 to HB5 fire at the step they guard, and a site report cannot be submitted without that day's toolbox meeting — the rule that makes the safety record a precondition of the production record.
  • The protocol reads, writes and decides under scoped tokens, and Administration is reachable only from a browser.

And one amber note: nothing notifies anyone. Every "Informed" entry in the RACI tables of Parts 2–9 is what the specification intends, not what happens today.

7. What is deliberately not on this page

  • Steps, records and states. Each box on the page is a chain that Parts 2–9 open into its individual steps with the record and state each one moves.
  • The RACI rows. The page shows who is Accountable (the gate badge). Responsible, Consulted and Informed for every step are in the part that owns it.
  • Hand-offs. Arrows are implied left to right. Every named hand-off — the places a process actually stalls — is drawn in the detail parts.
  • Messages. It is a channel, not a process, and it appears wherever a step's record has a thread. It has no lane of its own.
