## Payroll — test it properly, and mark loudly what you leave behind

Payroll has never been run. Nobody knows whether it works at all. Test it end to end:
create a period, look at the payroll lines, and try to run it.

To run it you must first enter and approve the four Philippine statutory rate tables —
Social Security System, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG and withholding tax. **You are allowed
to.** But you are entering numbers that real people could later be paid from, so make
them unmistakably fake:

- **One bracket per table**, never a realistic schedule.
- **Round, obviously wrong rates** — one percent, or a flat one peso. Never a number
  that could be mistaken for a real contribution rate.
- **Put `Z1-TEST` in every free-text field each table offers** — name, notes, reference.

Then, **at the very top of your report, before anything else**, write a plain warning
naming the four tables you approved and stating that real payroll must not be run until
they are replaced. Not buried in a list — the first thing on the page.

Record all of it: whether the period saved, whether the run guard fired, whether it
computed anything, whether payroll lines or payslips appeared, what the numbers were,
and the exact text of every message.

## Three experiments that matter more than the rest

These test rules the company wrote down and paid for. Do them carefully and report the
exact numbers you see. They are the reason this run is happening.

### Experiment A — is the approval gate set by the amount or by the severity?

The rule the company specified is that **the approval gate is derived from the amount
of money involved, and is never chosen by a person or by anything else.**

On your `Z1-` project, raise **four variation orders**:

| | Amount | Severity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | a small amount, around ₱50,000 | minor |
| 2 | a large amount, around ₱5,000,000 | minor |
| 3 | a small amount, around ₱50,000 | major |
| 4 | a large amount, around ₱5,000,000 | major |

For each one, record **which gate number it raised** and **which role it asked to
approve**. The gate number is shown on the record and on the approval request; you do
not need Administration to read it.

Then report the four results as a table. **Do not draw a conclusion — just report the
four numbers.** If the field names differ from what I have written, use the nearest
equivalent and say what you used.

### Experiment B — can a purchase order be raised before the contract is signed?

The company's sixth hard block is that **a signed contract and released funds are
required before any purchase order can be raised**, and before a project can leave
setup.

Do this in two parts:

1. **Before signing the contract** at step 7, try to raise a purchase order. Record
   whether it was refused, and **the exact wording of the refusal** — particularly
   whether it names a signed contract or names something else.
2. **After signing**, raise it again and record what happens.

Also try to move the project out of the setup stage before the contract is signed, and
record what happens.

### Experiment C — can you approve your own request?

The company's rule is that **nobody may approve their own request, anywhere, ever.**
You are signed in as the Chief Executive Officer, who has the most authority on the
platform, so if the rule holds for you it holds for everyone.

Raise something that needs approval, then try to approve it yourself. Record what
happens and the exact wording. Try this on at least two different kinds of record — a
variation order and a fund request, if you can reach both.
