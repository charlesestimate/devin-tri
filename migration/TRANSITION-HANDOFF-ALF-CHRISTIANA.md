## 1. Context — what this is and why you are doing it

Magnus runs its projects today on Facebook Messenger groups, spreadsheets and shared Google Drive folders. We have built one operations platform to replace all of that: the **Magnus Workspace Platform**, at

**https://magnus-solar-workspace-platform-446354.onhercules.app**

It covers pipeline, projects, design, procurement, permits, inventory, manpower, safety, operations and maintenance, documents, messages, finance, human resource, payroll and reports. Files uploaded to it are stored in the company Google Drive automatically. Conversations happen on the record they are about — a project, a purchase order, a site report — so that in three years, when someone asks what was agreed, the answer is findable.

The platform is being finished module by module. **What works today:** sign-in, people and roles, messaging (the General company space, group chats, direct messages, and a discussion thread on every project), file attachments in messages, and tasks. **What is still being rebuilt this week:** the operating modules — site reporting, procurement, finance, permits, inventory and payroll. **What comes after that:** loading the company's real records into the platform.

Your job sits in that order: get people on, get them talking on the platform instead of Messenger, test what is live, collect what is broken, and prepare the records for loading. You are not expected to fix bugs or build anything — you find, record, coordinate and report.

## 2. What done means

Your assignment is complete when you hand Karl the completion checklist in section 8 with every line ticked, and these three things are true:

1. **Every employee who should have an account has signed in and is using Messages daily.** Note carefully who *should*: office staff, department heads, project managers and each site's Person In Charge sign in. **Field crew members deliberately do not get accounts** — that is the design, not an oversight. They appear in attendance lists; they never log in. Do not chase crew accounts.
2. **The Messenger work groups are frozen.** Each active work group has moved to the platform, and the old Messenger group is renamed with the word ARCHIVED and no longer used for work.
3. **The bug list has zero open critical items.** Critical means: someone cannot sign in, a message or file is lost, someone sees information their role should not allow, or a screen cannot complete its basic job. Everything else is logged and scheduled, not necessarily fixed.

## 3. Part one — accounts and access

1. Ask Karl to grant your own sign-ins first (Administration → Persons → Grant Sign-In). Sign in, open Messages, and post one message each in General so you know the path works.
2. Build the access list: every office employee, department head, project manager and Person In Charge, with the email each will use. The platform already holds 19 person records; more can be added on the Persons screen. One row per person: name, email, role, site or department.
3. Karl or a console holder grants sign-in for each. As each person is granted, message them the platform address and ask them to sign in the same day and post one message in General. That first message is your proof they are in.
4. On a phone, the platform installs like an app: open the address in Chrome, menu, "Add to Home screen." Walk each field-facing person through this — the Person In Charge will live on the phone version.
5. Keep a simple tracker (a sheet is fine): person, email, granted date, first sign-in date, first message date. This tracker is part of your final report.

## 4. Part two — moving off Messenger

Do not try to move everyone at once. The rule that governs this whole part: **a channel that still works is a channel people keep using.** A group that is half moved is worse than one not moved at all.

1. **Pilot first.** Pick one active project group — choose the team most comfortable with phones. Create the matching space on the platform (the project already has its own thread; create a group only for talk that has no project). Move the members, announce in the old Messenger group that work talk now happens on the platform, and let it run for two or three days.
2. **Freeze, do not delete.** Once the pilot group is talking on the platform, rename the Messenger group to start with ARCHIVED, post a final pinned message pointing to the platform, and stop replying there. Never delete the group — the history stays readable.
3. **Repeat per group.** After the pilot holds, move the remaining groups one per day: department groups, then the other project groups. Same steps every time: create or identify the platform space, move members, announce, freeze.
4. **Coach the habits that differ from Messenger.** These will confuse people in the first week, so tell them up front: mention a person with @ when you need them — nobody is notified unless mentioned or messaged directly; talk about a project on the project's own thread, not in a general group; messages cannot be edited or deleted — a correction is a new message; there is no mute — if someone is getting noise, the fix is posting in the right thread, not muting.
5. **Track it:** group name, platform space, date moved, date frozen. Also part of your final report.

## 5. Part three — testing what is live

Spend your first two days using the platform hard, on both laptop and phone, and log everything odd. Work through this list and mark each pass or fail:

1. Sign in and out on laptop and phone; sign in again — nothing lost.
2. Post in General; confirm the other coordinator sees it without refreshing.
3. Direct message each other; confirm exactly one notification arrives, and only to the recipient.
4. Create a group; invite one person; confirm a third person cannot see the group exists.
5. Mention someone with @ — one notification; post without a mention — no notification.
6. Open a project from Projects and post on its thread; confirm the same message appears under Messages → Threads.
7. Attach a photograph and a document (a small file under 10 megabytes) to a message; download each back and open it; confirm with Karl that the file appeared inside the Magnus Workspace folder on the company Google Drive.
8. Convert a message to a task (the option on each message); confirm the task appears in My Day with the right assignee.
9. Try to edit or delete a sent message — it must not be possible.
10. Type while a dropdown is open, click rows once not twice, press Escape inside dialogs — note anywhere the screen misbehaves.
11. On the phone: count the taps from home screen to a sent message on a project thread. If it takes more taps than posting in Messenger, log it — that is a defect by our own standard.

**Do not test finance, site reports, procurement, inventory or payroll yet.** Those screens are being rebuilt as you read this; a test now would report failures on screens that are about to be replaced. Karl will hand you separate test packs (they exist already, named Set A, B and C) when those modules are ready, and other staff will run parts of them.

## 7. Bugs and reporting

**Where bugs go:** create one group on the platform named **Platform Bugs** with you two and Karl. Every bug is one message there: what you did, what you expected, what happened instead, screenshot attached, and which device. If the platform itself is down and you cannot post, use email as fallback, then copy it into the group when it returns.

**Severity, decided when you log it:** **Critical** — the definitions in section 2 item 3. **Major** — a feature does the wrong thing but work can continue another way. **Minor** — wording, layout, ampersands and abbreviations on labels (log these too; the platform's standard is full words), extra clicks.

**Daily:** one short end-of-day message from each of you in Platform Bugs, even if it only says "nothing new today": what moved, what is stuck, bug count by severity.

**Weekly to Karl:** the two trackers (access, Messenger groups), the sheet-collection status, and the open bug list.
