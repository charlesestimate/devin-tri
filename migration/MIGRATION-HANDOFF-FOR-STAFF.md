## 1. What this is about

Magnus is replacing the spreadsheets, Messenger groups and shared folders we run projects on with one operations platform. It covers pipeline, projects, design, procurement, permits, inventory, manpower, safety, operations and maintenance, documents, messages, finance, human resource, payroll and reports. It is being built now and will be tested by some of you on phones and at the Laguna warehouse in the coming weeks.

Before we can use it, the records we work with today have to be loaded into it. That loading is called the migration. **You will not type anything into the platform.** You collect the records in the form described below; the loading is done for you by an assistant working under my account, in batches, and each batch is checked on screen before the next.

Two rules from the platform's design govern this work:

1. **Nothing is loaded that nobody will maintain.** A record that is wrong on day one is worse than no record, because people will trust it. Give me what is true today, not what the old spreadsheet says.
2. **Live records only.** Finished projects, their documents and their chat history stay where they are and remain searchable. Historical accounting stays in the accounting system. Only projects, agreements, orders, permits and stock that are open today come across.

## 2. What you receive with this document

- **The migration workbook**, `MAGNUS-MIGRATION-TEMPLATES.xlsx`. Twenty-five sheets, one per type of record. Row 1 is the column names. Row 2 is an example in grey italics: overwrite it or delete it, never leave it. Hover over any column header to read the rule for that column. Yellow columns are required; light yellow columns are optional.
- **A Drive folder**, `Magnus Platform Migration`, with one sub-folder per department. Put your filled sheets and your documents there.

You do not have to retype anything that already exists in a spreadsheet. If your masterlist already has the information, put a copy of that file in your folder and write on the README sheet which file and which columns to read. The assistant will fill the workbook from it.

## 3. Rules for every sheet

- **Names link the sheets.** A person is referred to by exact full name everywhere; a customer by its legal name as on the contract; a site by the site name in the Sites sheet; a project by its project code; an item by its item code. Spell each one identically on every sheet. A link that does not match is refused.
- **Aliases prevent duplicates.** In the Persons sheet, list every spelling of a name that appears in old records, separated by semicolons: `M. Bautista; Mario B.`. Without this, one person becomes three records and payroll, attendance and deployment all break.
- **Dates** are written `2026-09-15`. **Money** is numbers only: `28800000`, no commas, no peso sign. **Yes and no** in lower case.
- **Where a column asks for a file name**, the file goes in your Drive folder with exactly that name. A signed contract means the signed copy, not a draft. An agreement without its signed document loads as draft and produces no billing.
- **Never put in the workbook:** passwords, monitoring portal logins, anybody's salary, ratings, disciplinary or medical information. Pay rates and statutory tables are entered on screen later by Human Resource and Finance together, under dual control.
- **Source reference.** Where the sheet has a `source_reference` column, write which file and row the record came from. This is how a wrong record is traced back.

## 6. Check your sheet before you submit

1. Every required (yellow) column is filled on every row.
2. Every name that links to another sheet is spelled exactly as it appears there.
3. No example row remains.
4. Every file name in a file column exists in your folder with that exact name.
5. Persons: no one appears twice; every alias spelling is listed.
6. Opening stock: the count is what you counted, not what the stock card says.
7. Projects: the workday counter and first site day are filled. Without them every migrated project will look as if it started on cutover day.
8. You have written the count of rows you filled on the README sheet against your sheet name.

## 7. What happens next

1. Your sheets and documents are validated. You will get back a short list of refused rows with the reason, if any. Fix and resubmit.
2. Records are loaded in batches, one sheet at a time, in the order printed on the README sheet. Each batch is checked on screen. A wrong batch is reversed and reloaded; this is expected and costs nothing.
3. The three warehouse custodians count, Cristy spot-checks, and the opening stock is locked per warehouse. After the lock, stock changes only by receipts, issues, transfers and counted adjustments.
4. On the cutover date, the old spreadsheets and folders are made read-only. Not deleted: read-only, so they can still be checked. The Messenger project groups are renamed and made read-only one project at a time.
5. Training follows by role. The Person In Charge training is the longest and happens on site, on your own phone, including how the app behaves with no signal.

## 8. What not to do

- Do not clean up or reorganise the old spreadsheets first. Give them as they are; the assistant reconciles.
- Do not create new codes or numbers for the platform. Use the codes you use today; the platform keeps them as your reference.
- Do not send anything by private message or email. Everything goes in the Drive folder so nothing is lost.
- Do not wait until the deadline to ask a question.
